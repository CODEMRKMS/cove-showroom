  What was built

  - Routes: vehicles CRUD, search, purchase, restock, soft delete, orders/mine,
    the admin events feed, health, ready, OpenAPI and Swagger UI, all through
    routes -> controllers -> services -> repositories.
  - Purchase is one guarded UPDATE ... WHERE quantity >= n inside a transaction
    that also writes the order and the PURCHASED event. Postgres row-locks it,
    so two buyers can't take the last unit. I proved the race test can fail: a
    temporary read-then-write version let all 10 buyers succeed against 3 units,
    and I reverted it.
  - Policy: authorize() exposes its key, and a test walks the real router. Every
    route must be public or guarded by its own ACCESS_POLICY entry, and every
    policy entry must have a route. ADR 0001 records admin-only POST/PUT.
    Removing the guard from DELETE made the test name that route.
  - Errors: one envelope with requestId, 500s that never leak (the real cause is
    logged server-side), body-parser errors mapped, unknown routes as JSON.
  - Seed: 24 demo vehicles (3 per category, 3 sold out), all marked "Demo
    listing."; idempotent, never revives deleted vehicles.
  - OpenAPI is hand-written and kept honest by a contract test: operations equal
    the real routes, security equals the policy map, and the documented fields
    equal real responses. Deleting one path, and separately one property, each
    failed the suite.

  Interview questions

  1. How do you know purchase can't oversell? One conditional UPDATE ... WHERE 
     quantity >= n in a transaction. The row lock makes a second buyer wait and
     re-check against the new value. A parallel test shows exactly M of N
     succeed, and a deliberately naive version fails it.
  2. How do you prove no route was left unprotected? authorize(key) tags its
     handler with the key. A test walks the real Express router, rebuilds each
     route's full path from the mount matcher, and fails if a non-public route
     lacks its own policy guard or a policy entry has no route.
  3. Why soft delete, and what happens to orders? Orders and ledger events hold
     foreign keys, so a hard delete would destroy history. Setting deletedAt
     hides the car everywhere. Orders keep their own snapshots of price and
     "Make Model", and the audit feed still names the vehicle.
